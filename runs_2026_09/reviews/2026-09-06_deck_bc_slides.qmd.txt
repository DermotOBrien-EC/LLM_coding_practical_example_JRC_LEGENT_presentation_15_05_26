## The same model, four months later {.tight}

::: {.smaller}
| Prompt | May 2026, Opus 4.7, one session | September 2026, Opus 4.7, three sessions |
|---|---|---|
| L1, 10 words | **10.8**, three naive baselines, no model fitted | **3.6, 3.3, 7.7**, a learned model in all three |
| L2, 46 words + `AGENTS.md` | **5.5**, one gradient-boosted model | **3.5, 2.9, 3.1**, LightGBM in all three |
| L3, 1,673 words | **3.4†** | **3.2, 3.5†**, the same construction, the same leak |
:::

::: {.takeaway}
The L1 result does not reproduce. Same model, same ten words, four months later: 3.3 to 7.7 instead of 10.8 (runs 1, 2, 3 in order). So most of what looked like "the models got better at beginner prompts" is not the model. What else changed: a different Claude Code build, headless instead of interactive, a clean sandbox, and three sessions instead of one. The experiment cannot separate them; a single session is simply not a measurement.
:::

::: {.source}
† The L3 winner reads the test week's own loads through its 24-hour lag and rolling features. The September Opus 4.7 runs repeat May's construction exactly, and land on May's number. One of them names the risk in its own methods write-up, then reports the number anyway.
:::

## Why L3 looks worse: the newer models stopped peeking {.tight}

::: {.smaller}
| Model given the 1,673-word prompt | Test MAPE | How the 24-hour lag was filled |
|---|---|---|
| Opus 4.7, May 2026 | 3.4 | the week's observed loads |
| Opus 4.7, September | 3.2, 3.5 | the week's observed loads |
| Opus 4.8 | 4.8, 4.6 | its own predictions |
| Opus 5 | 5.5, 5.5, 5.0 | its own predictions |
| Fable 5.1 | 5.0, 5.0, 5.5 | its own predictions |
| GPT-6-Astra (with the note) | 4.0, 5.1, 3.9 | its own predictions |
:::

::: {.takeaway}
Every model picks LightGBM and puts it first. The number depends on one thing the prompt does not specify: whether the lag it prescribes is filled with the model's own predictions or with the answer. The older model fills it with the answer, in May and again in September. The newer Claude models do not, and pay about 1.5 points for the honesty. GPT-6-Astra forecasts recursively too and still reaches 3.9, so the cost is not a law. The prompt's own feature list is what invites the mistake.
:::

## Three things the logs taught us {.tight}

:::: {.tipgrid .cols-3}
::: {#rerun01 .tipcard}
#### ACCURACY

Specificity buys honesty, not accuracy

Ten words got a frontier model to 3 to 4 percent. The 1,673 words scored 5: honest, week-ahead, and worse. The prompt's May-era feature list (a 24-hour lag) leads to recursive forecasting, where errors compound; the ten-word runs chose weekly lags and forecast directly. The older model filled that lag with the answer instead and scored 3.4. What specificity still buys: validation, intervals with measured coverage, a write-up, and no test-week inputs.
:::

::: {#rerun02 .tipcard}
#### REPEATABILITY

Same model class every time; the number moves by 0.5 to 3 points

Three L1 runs: LightGBM, LightGBM, LightGBM, MAPE 3.3 to 4.5. Three L2 runs: 2.3 to 5.4. Three L3 runs: LightGBM won every six-model bake-off, 5.0 to 5.5, Prophet second twice. Two of eight L1 and L2 sessions parked themselves on a background job; a one-line harness fix kept all four L3 sessions alive to the end, 71 to 129 minutes.
:::

::: {#rerun03 .tipcard .accent}
#### A SECOND MODEL

Harder work is not cleaner work, until the prompt says so

Opus 5 spent 31 to 93 minutes where Fable 5.1 spent 6 to 44, built more, and let the test week into a decision in five of its six L1 and L2 runs. Given the L3 prompt its headline was clean, like every Fable L3 headline. Across 90 sessions the pattern held: where a run cheats, it cheats while choosing, in sweeps that never reach the delivered script. Only the session log shows it.
:::
::::
